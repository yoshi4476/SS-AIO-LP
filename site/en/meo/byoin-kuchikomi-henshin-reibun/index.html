Review Replies for Hospitals and Clinics: Prohibited Expressions and 8 Sample Replies

In review replies for hospitals and clinics, 6 categories such as turning a reply into a patient testimonial, exaggerated claims, and comparative superiority claims violate the 医療広告ガイドライン (Medical Advertising Guidelines). This article covers prohibited expressions and safe rewordings, 8 sample replies for different situations, and an in-house approval process, based on information as of September 2026.

2 Principles to Settle First When Replying to Reviews at a Medical Institution

Most of the clinics that come to us for help through G-ran get stuck not on how well the reply is written, but on whether a given sentence crosses the line on confidentiality or advertising regulations. This article settles that judgment first, then turns it into wording you can use as is.

3 Sample Replies to Positive Reviews

Dear ◯◯, thank you for choosing our clinic.

2 Sample Replies for Low Ratings About Wait Times and Staff Attitude

Dear ◯◯, we are sorry to have kept you waiting.

2 Sample Replies to Reviews Alleging Misdiagnosis or Billing Trouble

Dear ◯◯, we are sorry for the worry and anxiety this has caused you.

How to Tell Factual Errors from Defamation, and How to Reply

A misunderstanding based on a patient's assumption and a malicious falsehood or abusive post are handled differently, even when both are low ratings. The former calls for a reply that carefully explains the facts; the latter is grounds for a removal request under Google's official policy on prohibited and restricted content.

6 Prohibited Expressions to Avoid in Replies

Why expressions that read like a patient testimonial are prohibited, and where the line falls for each of the 6 categories, is explained individually in our article on the 6 prohibited expressions under the 医療広告ガイドライン. Anyone drafting replies will find it easier to choose wording after reading through these 6 categories once.

Operating the Reply Process: Who Writes It, Who Approves It

Sitting in on in-house meetings at the clinics we support, I have repeatedly seen review replies left entirely to the goodwill of one particular front-desk staff member. Relying on a single person is exactly why a low rating goes unanswered on the days that person is out. Decide the division of roles in advance: the front desk or office manager drafts, and the director or a staff member who understands the advertising guidelines gives final approval.

How Review Reply Operations Differ Between Hospitals and Clinics

In what we have seen through G-ran, inappropriate replies to low ratings are overwhelmingly a clinic-side problem. The cause is not writing ability but a setup in which whoever wrote the reply can post it directly. Simply having the front desk read what the director drafted stops almost all aggressive wording.

FAQ

Can a hospital's review reply mention the details of treatment?

No. Referring to a specific symptom or treatment breaches confidentiality, so keep replies to thanks and general information.

Can low-rated reviews be deleted?

Not unless they violate policy. The practical approach is a careful, fact-based reply that shapes the impression for other readers.

Is it acceptable to write "we will definitely improve" in a reply?

Avoid it. A categorical guarantee of improvement risks counting as exaggerated advertising under the 医療広告ガイドライン.

How should we reply to a review alleging misdiagnosis?

Do not argue back. Direct the person to an in-house contact and avoid disputing the facts in public.

Who should write review replies?

The director or a staff member who understands the advertising guidelines should write them, with a second person checking before they go live.
